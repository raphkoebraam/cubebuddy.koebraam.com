Cube Buddy Press

Cube Buddy teaches the 3x3 cube on a cube you can turn, then times your solves.

Cube Buddy is a cube tutor and speedcubing timer for iPhone and iPad. The beginner method is taught in seven stages on an interactive 3D cube: each step lights up the pieces it moves, plays the moves one at a time or in one go, and explains why; a notation trainer teaches the move language with the cube itself. The Play tab holds a cube to turn by hand, competition scrambles, a scanner that reads a physical cube through the camera, and Solve, which walks the user out of any scramble with the same stages as the lessons. The timer offers WCA scrambles, inspection, +2 and DNF, sessions, averages, personal bests and CSV export and import. Everything runs on the device: no account, no advertising, no analytics. The beginner method and every tool are free; Cube Buddy Premium adds the speedcubing algorithm sets (F2L, OLL, PLL) as guided lessons, as a monthly or yearly subscription.

What sets it apart

  • Lessons on a live 3D cube: the pieces of every step are highlighted and each move plays on the cube, step by step or at once.
  • A coach on the device: ask why a move works and get an answer from Apple Intelligence when available, built-in explanations otherwise.
  • Your cube, not a demo: scan it with the camera or paint it on the net, and Solve takes that exact state through the method.
  • The speedcubing sets taught, not listed: every F2L, OLL and PLL case as a guided lesson with spotlights, left-handed mirrors and training scrambles.
  • A complete timer: WCA scrambles with preview, inspection with voice alerts, sessions, ao5, ao12, ao100, trend and distribution charts, a daily scramble, CSV in and out.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics, no servers; the camera frames never leave the phone.
  • VoiceOver reads the cube, the scrambles and the lessons; Dynamic Type throughout.

About the developer

Raphael Koebraam is a software engineer who builds Cube Buddy alone. He made it because every cube tutorial shows the moves on someone else's cube: here the cube is in the app, the pieces that matter light up, and the same explanations work on the cube on your desk.
